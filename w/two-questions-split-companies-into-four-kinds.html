AI Market Structure 2026-05-27

Two questions split companies into four kinds

Every management team is quietly answering two questions right now (even if they never say them out loud).

Is AI a tailwind or a headwind for us? Are we actually AI native, or not?

Those two questions explain most of the AI layoff news flooding the feed. The answers split the world into four kinds of company.

AI native with a tailwind: you took the red pill, you were built to fly, none of this is news to you.

Companies going through an "AI transformation" are the crowded category. They have a tailwind but weren't built AI native, so they transform their DNA while growing. That usually shows up as layoffs, dressed up with efficiency metrics.

The most challenging corner is SaaSpocalypse: not AI native, facing a headwind. It's where you'll secretly meet most of the large tech companies. Publicly, 90% will tell you AI is a tailwind. In the boardroom, most admit it's a headwind. This is where the AI washing lives. Most recent tech layoffs point at AI while AI quietly eats the core business.

Here's what the matrix hides. The categories move, and some companies are genuinely debatable, whether they've actually transformed or just say they have. ClickUp insists it took a structural AI bet, agents running the work. Plenty of observers think it's still in the corner.

AI native today is just the baseline tomorrow. Once the market migrates, 100x velocity stops being an edge and becomes the starting line.

A test I heard for how AI native you really are: from the moment Claude goes down, how long until half the company starts crying? Minutes means native. The longer it takes, the bigger the transformation ahead.

Still working out where this leads. But the line that matters is the one between catching a tailwind and rebuilding the body. Grove Ventures
